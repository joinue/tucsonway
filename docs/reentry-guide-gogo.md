## Getting Started — Key Tips

- **Do your homework.** Write to agencies *before* release to find out requirements (most don't accept walk-ins from prison).
- **Give yourself time to adjust.** Patience matters — feeling depressed or overwhelmed is normal.
- **Ask for help.** Keep asking until you get the right answer — it doesn't make you weak.
- **Sex offender note:** Programs listed do NOT accept sex offenders unless explicitly stated.
- **TB cards** are no longer required for admission to Pima County shelters.
- If paroled: make immediate contact with parole office; apply for AHCCCS + SNAP via DES ASAP — many services on this list require AHCCCS.
- If Native American: tribal services may also apply.
- Free library computer/internet access available.

## Independent Housing — Application Tips

**Bring to applications/interviews:**
- Parole officer's contact info
- Proof of current employment
- 2–3 personal references (notify them in advance)

**Low-cost search tools:**
- **pimacountyhousingsearch.org** — updated every 2 weeks; computer access at any Pima County library
- **Pima County Housing Center** (a.k.a. **El Banco**) — 801 W. Congress, Tucson, AZ 85745 — (520) 724-2460

**Tucson Crime-Free Multi-Housing Program:** Properties participating in this may refuse renters with felony records. Acceptance depends on the crime. **Ask before paying any application fee** — no legal challenge is known.

### Identification

- Most releasing inmates can obtain a replacement Driver's License / State ID *before* release
- Otherwise an ADC ID card is issued at release
- Within **15 calendar days** of release, can get AZ State ID at any MVD using the affidavit of incarceration — **fee $12** (waived if age 65+)
- After 15 days you'll need two other forms of verification
- MVD phone — Phoenix: (602) 255-0072 / **Tucson: (520) 629-9808** / Elsewhere AZ: 1-800-251-5866

### Reducing Overdose Risk

- **Use slow, use less** — fentanyl overdoses can occur before injection finishes
- **Snort/smoke instead of injecting** when possible (lower risk)
- **Space out doses** — fentanyl acts fast, varies by tolerance
- **Don't use alone** — have someone check on you; group? **stagger use**
- **Test it** — knowing what's in drugs reduces risk
- **Always carry naloxone**; know overdose signs
- **Listen to your body** — hydrate, eat, rest
- **Never Use Alone Hotline: 877-696-1996**

### Tips for Discussing Background

**Be honest about convictions.** If asked "Have you *ever* been convicted of a felony/misdemeanor?" disclose **all**. If asked "in the last 7 years?" and yours was 8 years ago, the answer is **NO**.

**Sample disclosures:**
- "Non-violent drug conviction – 2006"
- "Fraudulent schemes – 2005"
- "DUIs – 2005, 2008"

**Pair with positive change statements:**
- "Since then I've completed [certificate, sobriety, job assignments, volunteered]"
- "I have been sober for over [X]"
- "I am committed to living an honest life"
- "I put it behind me and redirected my life"
- "My goal is to help others avoid the mistake I made"

**Avoid** the words "aggravated" or "dangerous" (interpreted as violent).
**Do not** write a letter explaining your felony. Only cover letters, letters of recommendation, and reference letters.
