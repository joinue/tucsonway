# Poverello House of Tucson

A **daytime shelter and support house for adults experiencing homelessness**, run in the Franciscan tradition of hospitality. Guests come in off the street for a day to sit down, decompress, get cleaned up, and have healthy meals in a homelike environment.

**Poverello House does NOT offer overnight services.**

## Services

- **Daytime rest** — a safe, welcoming, homelike place to kick up your feet, relax, or visit with friends
- **Food** — healthy meals throughout the day
- **Showers** — available throughout the day
- **Laundry** — washing machines so guests leave with clean clothes

> In need of overnight shelter? Poverello House points guests to the broader Tucson shelter system (see the Directory).

## History

The name comes from St. Francis of Assisi — villagers called him *"Il Poverello"* (the little poor man) — and "Poverello" has been used for ministries with the poor in the Franciscan tradition for centuries.

- **October 29, 2008** — Poverello House of Tucson opened its doors, inviting homeless men in off the street for the day.
- Founded by **Br. David Buer, OFM**, a Franciscan Brother who ministered to the homeless for many years. He encountered the Poverello House concept in San Francisco's Tenderloin in the early '90s and established two Poverello Houses in Nevada before being assigned to Tucson in 2005.
- Moved to a larger house at **267 E. Blacklidge Dr.** and expanded services to women after the move.
